## Target-player sessions — GDD Section 21.3

Recruit eight first-time newcomers, eight cyber generalists who are not hunting specialists, and three specialist credibility reviewers. Use anonymous participant codes. Do not let later participants watch earlier sessions or learn their case answer. Assign different M01 variations; a researcher may arrange synthetic save fixtures without exposing private variant codes to players.

1. Start from the live home screen on the chosen device, with no account and audio silent. Let the participant choose assistance. Give an uncoached first-run segment; record the first meaningful action, first discovery, first frustration, blockers and elapsed time before helping.
2. Observe source choice, comparisons, citation, an alternative explanation, uncertainty/confidence and a scoped recommendation. Ask why they chose a source after recording the action. Do not tell them which ending is correct.
3. After closure, ask them to explain their finding and what could not be known. Ask why an unusual login is insufficient by itself and why an unresolved, visibility-limited case can be good work. Record their own explanation.
4. Offer another variation without pressure. Record whether they actually begin, separately from a verbal “would play again.” A same-world replay is available for trying a different investigative route and retains the completed case separately.
5. Ask whether it felt like investigating or filling in a form. Ask them to name the discovery that changed or bounded their view. Rate home art and actual in-game presentation separately, on 1–5 scales.
6. Offer optional voice listening and a muted comparison. Record consent/decline, clips used, clarity, distraction, volume, pronunciation, caption/transcript use and mute choices. Silence must never remove a fact or action.

## Physical device and access matrix

Record exact current/previous major browser versions at the time of testing. Linux WebKit is engine automation, not physical Safari. Phone emulation is not an iPhone test.

- Desktop: Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari; keyboard-only start → acquire → cite → alternate/limits → review → close → export/resume; visible focus and back/escape behaviour where applicable.
- iPadOS Safari: portrait and landscape; touch targets, panel reflow, rotation during a pending check, software keyboard/notes, case closure and replay.
- iPhone Safari portrait and Android Chrome portrait: 320px-equivalent/relevant device widths, 200% text, browser bars/safe areas, scrolling with captions/settings, note entry and tap accuracy.
- VoiceOver/TalkBack and a desktop screen reader: source limitations, acquisition references, caveats, selected states, review confirmation, save/error announcements and accessible transcripts. Enable screen-reader-friendly audio; no competing unsolicited speech.
- Media: fresh-session consent, phone speakers/headphones/low volume, blocked playback then retry, mute mid-line, skip/replay, background/return, interruption and rotation with captions. No unexpected backlog or time advancement.
- Storage: private session, quota pressure, browser eviction/user clearing, abrupt OS kill after committed and unsaved work, multiple tabs/ownership, old-format migration and damaged backup. Verify stored state, not just labels. Export before destructive device-storage tests.
- Performance: name the reference phone and iPad; at least 100 ordinary interactions, evidence-panel timings, short-case commits, cold core load under declared network conditions and long-task traces. Lab report is a comparison, not physical acceptance.
